Compliance lives in the bond token, not in the app. The app and the API only ask the token and, on testnet,
help you get on its list.

## The officer's rights

The compliance officer holds the token's KYC, freeze and pause roles.

| Action | Token call | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Grant KYC | `grantKyc(account, vcId, validFrom, validTo, issuer)` | The address may hold and transfer the bond |
| Revoke KYC | `revokeKyc(account)` | Fills to or from the address are refused with `InvalidKycStatus` |
| Freeze an address | `setAddressFrozen(account, true)` | The address goes on the control list; every transfer involving it is refused with `AccountIsBlocked`, KYC or not |
| Unfreeze | `setAddressFrozen(account, false)` | Removed from the control list |
| Pause | `pause()` | Every transfer of the token is refused with `IsPaused` |

The **Compliance** page in the app does all of this for whichever bond token you pick, and shows, for any
address, the KYC status, the freeze state and the token's live answer to `canTransferFrom`.

## The testnet KYC desk

On testnet you should not need to find a human. The API holds the officer key and grants KYC to anyone who
proves they control an address:

<Steps>
  <Step title="You sign a message">
    The app asks your wallet to sign `Bond Desk testnet KYC request for <address> at <unix minute>`. No gas is
    spent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The API checks it">
    The signature must match the address and be at most five minutes old. Three requests per hour per address
    and per IP, one in-flight transaction per address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The officer bot acts">
    For every bond token: `addIssuer` if the bond issuer is not yet registered, then `grantKyc` with a ten-year
    validity, the same arguments the issuance script used.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Revocation works the same way with the message `Bond Desk testnet KYC revocation for <address> at <unix
minute>`. The issuer's own KYC cannot be revoked through the desk.

<Warning>
  The desk is a testnet convenience, not a compliance model. In production the officer is a person or a KYC
  provider, and nobody grants themselves KYC. What does not change is that the token enforces the result at every
  transfer.
</Warning>

## A freeze in practice

On 2026-09-12 at 12:00 UTC the deployed monitor froze bond 1 from the CRE network. A KYC'd investor with a valid
order could not fill it: the market reverted `BondNotActive(1, Frozen)` before the token was even asked. The
admin later unfroze the bond. Both receipts are on the [receipts](/reference/receipts) page. A frozen **bond** is
a registry status set by the risk gate; a frozen **address** is the token's control list set by the officer.
They are separate gates and both live in contracts.
